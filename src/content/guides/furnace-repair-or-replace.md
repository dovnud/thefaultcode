## DOE signs it may be time to replace
From the DOE Energy Saver guide, consider replacing your heating system if:
| Sign | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Furnace or boiler more than 15 years old | Older equipment may be less efficient |
| Frequent repairs and rising energy bills | Ongoing costs add up |
| Some rooms too hot or too cold | Possible system or duct issues |
| Humidity problems or excessive dust | Comfort and air quality issues |
| Noisy heating or cooling system | Possible mechanical wear |

## Things to compare
- **AFUE:** DOE suggests asking for high annual fuel utilization efficiency (AFUE) when replacing. ENERGY STAR lists certified furnaces.
- **The fault:** some codes point to inexpensive parts (filters, sensors), others to boards, motors or the heat exchanger. Your code page lists the likely causes.
- **Safety findings:** a technician may find a cracked heat exchanger or venting problem. ENERGY STAR notes a cracked heat exchanger can cause the equipment to operate less safely.

## Before deciding
Ask for the diagnosis in writing, including which part failed and why. Regular maintenance helps too: ENERGY STAR recommends a heating check-up each fall.

> **Safety first:** If you smell gas, don't touch light switches or use a phone inside. Leave the building and call your gas utility from outside. Turn off power to the furnace before opening any panel, and never bypass or jumper a safety switch. Gas, venting, wiring and control-board work is for a licensed HVAC technician.
